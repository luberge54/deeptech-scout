# Calibration worksheet — score these five by hand

## Read this first

**Fill this in before running the pipeline, and before looking at any model output.** Once a
score generated by the model has been seen, the comparison is worthless — the anchoring effect
is real and it will pull the hand-scores toward the machine's. This worksheet only has value if
it is completed blind.

Budget roughly 20 minutes per company: their website, their news or blog page, and a quick
search for recent coverage. Do not research exhaustively — the point is to reproduce the same
conditions the pipeline works under, not to beat it with extra effort.

Where evidence is genuinely absent, apply the same rule the model gets: **cap the score at 2
and mark confidence LOW.** Do not guess from what seems plausible for a company of that size.

---

## Why these five

The calibration set is not a random sample. Each company was chosen to stress a specific way
the pipeline could fail.

| Company | What it tests |
|---------|---------------|
| **ANYbotics** | Baseline. Heavily documented, mature, plenty of public evidence. If the model and I disagree here, something is broken at the foundation. |
| **Humanoid** | Does the model conflate funding with traction? Very large raise, very young company, unlikely to have fleet deployments. Traction should score low despite the headline numbers. |
| **Verity** | Mid-range with genuine named customers. Tests whether the pipeline can find real deployment evidence when it exists but is not loudly advertised. |
| **mimic robotics** | Founded 2024, thin public footprint. Tests the missing-evidence rule — this should produce low-confidence flags, not invented scores. |
| **Gravis Robotics** | Minimal public coverage. Tests the floor. If the model produces confident scores here, it is hallucinating. |

That reasoning is itself a talking point: the calibration set was designed to find specific
failure modes, not to check a box.

---

## Anchor reference

Keep this visible while scoring. Scores 2 and 4 sit between the anchors.

| Criterion | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|-----------|---|---|---|
| **Field traction** (30) | Demos, pilots, LOIs only. No paying deployment. | Paying deployments at ~3–10 customers, each install custom. | Repeat orders, tens of units in daily service, at least one customer past pilot into a fleet. |
| **Team / execution** (25) | First-time founders, no hardware shipped, flat team. | Credible technical founders, one generation shipped, steady hiring. *ETH/EPFL pedigree caps here.* | Shipped hardware at scale before, or 2+ generations with shortening cycles; pulls senior talent from big tech. |
| **Technology** (20) | Reproducible with off-the-shelf parts; capability already in others' published research. | Real engineering depth, but the approach is broadly available. | Compounds with use: proprietary data flywheel, unbuyable component, or multi-year certification. |
| **Market** (15) | Narrow niche, or no existing budget line for this spend. | Sizeable but crowded, or procurement cycles long enough to starve a startup. | Large segment, urgent *and already budgeted* pain, buyer purchases in this category today. |
| **Timing** (10) | Could have been built five years ago and wasn't; no external change explains now. | Rides the general Physical AI wave. **Default.** | A specific dateable unlock: regulation, component price threshold, customer mandate. |

**Weighted total:** for each criterion, `score × weight ÷ 5`. Sum the five. Result is out of 100.
Example: traction 4 → `4 × 30 ÷ 5 = 24`.

### What to write up afterwards

- **Mean absolute gap** across the five, and whether the model runs systematically high or low
- **Which criterion disagrees most.** A prediction to check: market and field traction, since
  both depend on judgment that public text supports poorly
- **Any case where the model was confident and wrong** — the most important finding in the whole
  project, and the one worth leading with
- **Any case where the model was right and I was wrong**, stated plainly. It is more convincing
  than a report where the human always wins
